## 0. The one-line reframe (the biggest takeaway)

**The `/developers` funnel is not a SaaS product sold to developers. The estate page + market
signalling is a free WEDGE.** The business behind it is the Seafields shape:

> F2K controls the land → shares the land-value uplift → sells modular homes into the estate →
> (pays introducers where they have no natural downstream cut).

So the page must sell the **outcome** (a smoother, de-risked, funded path to a viable project) and
the **deal shape**, not a feature list of portals. The portals are how it runs; they are not why a
developer says yes.

**Evidence from the three real engagements (not slides):**
- **Seafields** — self-generated via relationship (Uwe, Humphries, Thompson). The real gap was "no
  coherent strategy." F2K stepped in, controls the land, shares uplift, sells modular in.
- **Dutton Terrace** — the **agent** saw the estate page as a cheap way to get market signals and is
  driving it; more sites coming from the same agents.
- **360 Millers Road** — owner + their agent see it as a great market-signalling move.
- No one is paying for the page yet: F2K is in build mode, testing the generation system and getting
  reps on live estates. Correct call for now.

## 1. The honed ICP

**Primary ICP: the land-rich, strategy-poor non-builder.** An owner or boutique / "accidental"
developer who has a **new residential site with ≥20-dwelling potential** but is missing the half F2K
supplies: a coherent development + go-to-market strategy, real market signal, cost-certain homes, and
a funding path. They do **not** build their own homes at scale.

Two shapes (already reflected in the page's Path A / Path B):
- **Land subdivider (land-only):** sells lots, not homes. F2K positions the lots alongside real home
  options to show the land's true development potential, and helps sell if signals prove up.
- **Developer with dwellings in the masterplan:** F2K offers the modular alternative to the
  traditional build plan they may have (cost certainty, faster delivery).

**Qualifying test for a good-fit estate** (use this to triage inbound):
1. New residential site, ≥20 dwellings of potential.
2. Owner/developer owns or can control the land.
3. Lacks a coherent, proven GTM/strategy, or has an unproven masterplan.
4. Does **not** have their own volume home-building capability.
5. Open to F2K acting as estate manager + supplying modular homes.

## 2. Premise challenge — Metricon / GemLife are a red herring for THIS funnel

Everything in the model depends on the counterparty **not** already having their own homes, capital,
GTM, and strategy. That is precisely why land-rich, strategy-poor owners lean in.

- **Metricon** *is* the builder F2K's modular homes would displace. It will not buy F2K homes or hand
  over land control.
- **GemLife** has its own capital, land-lease model, and buyer engine. It does not need F2K to
  de-risk or fund.

Listing them as the aspirational top of the funnel quietly drags the page toward a generic "platform
for all developers" pitch — the exact muddiness flagged in the brief.

**CONFIRMED (2026-07-03):** **Option 2 — focus now, park volume builders as a separate future lane.**
ICP is the land-rich/strategy-poor non-builder. Volume/value builders and land-lease operators are
OUT of this funnel, but kept as a *future, different* relationship where F2K is a **modular supplier /
subcontractor** (no land control, different deal, different surface). Revisit trigger: an inbound
volume-builder who wants F2K modular supply — handle as its own lane, not via `/developers`.

## 3. The distributor / channel map (the growth engine)

The repeatable engine is not one funnel; it is a **network of introducers**, each touching the
developer at a different moment. F2K already split the surfaces (`/developers`, `/agents`).

**Reward rule (the principle that makes every funnel stand):** the introducer is paid in *their own
downstream revenue where they have it, and an F2K-funded benefit where they don't.*

| Funnel | Who | What's in it for them | Cost to F2K | Attribution / trust mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct developer** | Owner/dev finds us or we approach them | A smoother, de-risked, funded path to a viable project | — | F2K **appoints** the selling agents (exclusive, or max 2 agencies) — the sales channel is F2K's carrot |
| **Agent introduction** | RE agent brings a site | **Guaranteed sales exclusivity** on the estate; hero to their vendor; a process they can track | Listing they'd want anyway | Intro = locked exclusivity, recorded so F2K never goes direct around them |
| **Third party** (civil e.g. Heath/HAS, trades, related pros) | Sees developers early (e.g. at civil-works enquiry) | Their **own** downstream work (earthworks) **plus an F2K-funded introducer payment** if the project proves up | Introducer fee (built into the deal) | Introducer registered against the site |

**Structural requirement (load-bearing):** the F2K "model deal" template must carry an
**introducer-payment factor**, paid on prove-up, so third-party funnels are economically real and not
goodwill. Agents self-fund (their sales commission); civil/trades do not, so F2K must.

**Channel-conflict guard (do not skip):** a site is attributed to **whoever registers it first**, and
that attribution governs both sales exclusivity and introducer payment. Without a visible, trackable
attribution mechanic, the first time an agent feels F2K "went direct" around them, the channel
poisons and stops feeding sites. (Partly exists already — see the `?ref=` referral-attribution work;
may need a formal site-registration ledger.)

## 4. What this means for each surface

- **`/developers`** — sell the **outcome + the deal shape**, not the portal feature list. Keep the
  estate definition and the two paths. Add a crisp "what's in it for you" and a plain "how the F2K
  estate model works" (F2K as estate manager, land-value uplift share, modular supply, funding
  support). Drop the Metricon-style aspirational breadth. Keep the *commercial specifics* (uplift %,
  fees) behind the conversation, not on the public page.
- **`/agents`** — lead with **"bring us a site, lock your sales exclusivity,"** plus the trackable
  prove-up process and the two-directional carrot (F2K also appoints agents onto sites it sources).
- **Third-party introducers (civil/trades like HAS)** — either a new `/partners` surface or a section
  on `/agents`: "introduce a developer, and if it proves up you get [downstream work] + an F2K
  introducer payment." This is the funnel with no page today.
- **Every surface** — the missing ingredient across all of them was a differentiated, concrete
  **"what's in it for you."** That is the fix.

## 9. Deal economics — the loaded base-rate + uplift model (2026-07-03)

Minimum **20 dwellings**. **F2K has three independent income streams:**
1. **Project-management fee** — for running the process. Recovered in the base rate → F2K is paid for
   the work regardless of the upside.
2. **Share of the land-value uplift** — the profit *above* the base rate, split with the developer.
3. **Full margin on the modular homes F2K generates** — 100% to F2K, separate from the land.

**The "base rate"** (the land base agreed with the developer) is a fully-loaded cost floor:
> land acquisition + soft costs already spent + interest on the developer's sunk capital + infra/
> servicing to title + F2K PM fee + introducer fee(s) + agent sales commission (~2%+) + other deal costs.

**Market price = base rate, uplifted** — by a % or by local market comparables. That is the **public
price shown on the estate page**, and the waitlist/registrations are the market-signalling test of
whether buyers bite at it. Bite → proceed; weak signal → adjust (lower uplift / re-mix product) or
reject. This makes the stage-5 go/adjust/reject gate quantitative. Everyone's costs + fees are
recovered in the base *before* profit-sharing, so every party is de-risked and the uplift is clean
shared upside.

**Introducer fee — reframed (supersedes §8.1's "out of F2K's value capture"):** it is a **line in the
base rate, recovered from the sale proceeds at settlement**, NOT a charge against F2K's margin. The
price carries it, so F2K can publish a fair standard rate without eroding its return.
- *Recommended shape:* a **flat $ per dwelling** (a civil/trades partner can instantly compute
  "N lots × $X"), kept modest (every $ lifts the required market price), paid at settlement.
- ⚠️ **OPEN — Dennis sets the figure.** Starting frame (NOT a decision): property finder/introducer
  fees typically ~1–2% of land value, or a flat few-hundred-to-~$1k per lot.

**Three disciplines (so the model holds):**
1. **The loaded base rate is the break-even.** Every fee stacked in raises the price the market must
   clear; if it pushes past comps/signals, the uplift evaporates → no-go. The signalling gate enforces
   this, so the stack must stay lean.
2. **Agent commission is a % of the *achieved* sale price** (a deduction from top-line realisation),
   mechanically different from the fixed-$ cost lines. Model it as a deduction, not a fixed base line,
   to avoid circularity (commission depends on price, price depends on the base).
3. **Over-held / over-capitalised sites are an ICP disqualifier.** Big sunk costs + interest inflate
   the base rate and it may not prove up against market comps. The base rate must be sane relative to
   end market value.

## 10. Deriving the base rate — feasibility + QS + valuation (= the funder pack) (2026-07-03)

The base rate (§9) must be **objective and funder-recognised**, not a guess. Three professional inputs
produce it, and the same three ARE the funder pack (internal JV partners or external funders need
exactly these). One set of work, two jobs.

| Input | Provider | Contributes to base rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Civil feasibility + indicative costs** | Civil engineer (e.g. Porter Consulting — $4,800 for the feasibility/indicative-cost items on Seafields) | Infra/servicing cost line + **risk-catcher** | Porters flagged real Seafields servicing risks (drainage/POS reallocation, sewer-extension distance, water-trunk crossing) that can blow a regional base rate. This is stage-1 "feasible + allowed," quantified. |
| **QS cost plan** | Quantity surveyor (separate discipline) | Bankable total-development cost | NOT the same as the engineer's *indicative* cost. Use the indicative for stage-1 signalling; commission a formal QS only at **firm-up / funder pack**. Don't pay for a full QS before demand is proven. |
| **Registered valuation** | Independent valuer | "As-is" land value (base) + projected **end values** (the market-price / uplift target, comp-backed) | The number funders require; both stands the base rate and validates the uplift. Porters can't give this. |

**Cost-exposure policy (no-uncovered-cost rail):**
- **Civil feasibility/design/works → the civil JV partner funds it** (Seafields: HAS engages Porters
  directly), recovers cost + margin via the split. F2K carries nothing on the civil side.
- **Valuation (+ any non-civil QS) → developer pays, OR F2K recovers it as a base-rate soft-cost line
  on prove-up.** Avoid F2K funding it on spec; in build mode F2K *may* underwrite the first few to get
  reps, but that's a conscious, logged exception, not policy.

**Process implication — a new stage.** Insert **"Feasibility & Valuation → Base Rate"** right after
listing (the quantitative version of stage-1 "prove up the plan"): feasibility + indicative cost +
valuation → base rate → base rate + uplift → public price → the price market-signalling tests. QS +
refreshed valuation come at **firm-up** for the funder pack.
