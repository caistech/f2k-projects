### Hero — "Have an estate in mind? Let's build it together."

- Clean, confident, and it reads to a developer, not a consumer. "Tell us about your project — your vision, your goals and how you like to do deals" is a nice, low-friction open. I know within five seconds what you want me to do.
- The line I actually stopped on is one screen down: **"We prove your estate up before anyone spends big."** That's the right hook for my world. De-risking before civil spend is the whole game. You've led with the thing developers lose sleep over, not with "modular homes." Good instinct.
- Commercial nitpick: "how you like to do deals" is warm but tells me you'll shape the deal around me, which a cynic reads as "we haven't decided how we get paid." I'll come back to this — it's the running theme.
- Opportunity: add one line of proof to the hero — "3 live estates, 145 lots on-platform in WA/SA/TAS" — so the credibility I only discovered by digging into the Estates menu is visible before I scroll.

### The 5-stage process — "How it works, end to end"

- This is the strongest section on the page. The narrative — prove the plan is *allowed* on the site (zoning/planning/constraints) → build the page → engage local agents → gather waitlist + read signals → go/adjust/stop, then activate funders — is exactly how a disciplined developer thinks. Stage 5 offering "go, **adjust**, or stop" is honest; most pitches only have a "go" gear.
- Now the operator's problem, and it's the big one. Stage 4 says you "capture waitlist registrations and cross-check them against genuine market signals — real buyer intent, not guesswork." But your own top-of-page banner says, in red, **"No deposit is required or accepted."** So what you're gathering is soft expressions of interest. A financier does not fund against soft EOIs. When I take a project to a bank for a construction facility, they want **qualified, deposit-backed presales** — signed contracts, 10% deposits in trust, often with finance-approved buyers, hitting a debt-cover ratio. A waitlist of "I'm interested" names, however well-tracked, is a marketing metric, not a funding condition. Right now the page implies the waitlist *is* the demand proof that unlocks funders (Stage 5), and I don't believe that leap. Tell me how a registration becomes a bankable presale and you've closed the most important gap on the page.
- Terminology: you use "waitlist registrations," "genuine market signals," and "real buyer intent" as if they're interchangeable with presales. To a developer they are three different strength-levels of commitment. Be precise about which one you deliver and when.
- Opportunity: add a stage (or a sentence in Stage 4/5) on **converting registrations to EOIs to deposit-backed contracts** — even "we move interested buyers through holding deposits / EOIs held in trust as the project firms up." That single mechanism is what turns your platform from a marketing site into a funding instrument.

### The two pillars — "A whole platform and a home pipeline"

- **The estate platform.** I clicked into Seafields to test whether "portal" meant something or nothing. It's real: interactive lot map, live stages (20/1/24 open, others locked), per-lot pricing, size bands, a modular home catalogue with prices, purchase terms. That's a genuine sell-down tool, not a brochure. The agent/developer/funder-portal claims are therefore credible to me because I saw the buyer-facing half working. That earns trust.
- **The funder portal / data room.** This is the one I specifically went hunting for, because publicly advertising a "funder data room" usually means someone's cost stack and margins are one guessed URL away. Credit where due: `/dataroom` bounced me straight to a magic-link login (`/dataroom/login`). Numbers are behind auth, funder-by-funder, exactly as the copy claims ("only authorised funders you approve can see the numbers"). That's the right architecture and it directly answers my exposure worry. Keep it that way — never let a feasibility PDF sit on a public estate page.
- **The modular home pipeline / cost certainty.** This is your differentiator and you undersell the proof. "Factory-built for cost certainty and predictable delivery timelines" is the promise every developer wants, but the page gives me the adjective and not the mechanism. Cost certainty to me means a *fixed-price build contract* with a defined escalation regime and a named party carrying the risk. Is F2K the builder, or a coordinator in front of a builder? Is the price fixed at contract or indexed? Who wears cost overruns and program blowouts — you, the factory, or me? Until I know that, "cost certainty" is marketing, and I've been burned by "fixed price" that wasn't.
- Commercial nitpick: "One coordinated partner across land, homes and delivery" is attractive operationally but it's also you telling me you sit across *all three*. Read alongside the terms page (below), that's a lot of control concentrating in F2K. Not a dealbreaker — but say it out loud rather than let me infer it.
- Opportunity: put a "cost certainty, concretely" box on the page — fixed-price at contract, escalation capped/indexed to X, delivery window Y weeks from slab, F2K/factory carries build risk. One honest paragraph here converts more developers than the whole platform pitch, because it's the thing only you can offer and everyone else fudges.

### Getting started + the commercials I couldn't find

- The "getting started is simple / all you need to do today is tell us about it" framing is well-judged for a first touch. Low commitment, clear next action.
- But here's where I, as the person signing, want the deal shape and it isn't on the page: **how does F2K actually get paid?** I chased it. The "estate submission terms" link (good that it's a real link, not buried plain text) is honest and plain-English: submission is free, F2K earns "by controlling the land programme and delivering the housing," specific structure (outright / JV / staged) agreed per project. That's a fair basis-of-engagement and I respect the candour. What it still doesn't give me is any *number or range*: no indicative margin on the homes, no JV split band, no coordination fee, nothing. And "Factory2Key acts as estate manager — leading the project, lot allocations, management and delivery" is a significant handover of control that a developer is agreeing to by ticking a required checkbox. I'd want that conversation before I tick, not after.
- Opportunity: a short "How F2K is remunerated" section (even ranges, even "typically we make our return on the home delivery, land structure varies") would pre-qualify serious developers and stop tyre-kickers wasting your time. Right now the money conversation is entirely deferred to a call, which filters *out* exactly the experienced operators who won't enquire without it.

### Morgan, the voice guide + the enquiry form

- Morgan works. On my machine the voice mode showed "Not supported" (no mic in my setup) and it fell back cleanly to a text chat — that's the right behaviour, degrade don't fake. I asked her point-blank: "12ha near Busselton, zoned residential — how does F2K get paid and do I keep control of the land?" She gave a sensible, in-character answer (flexible structures, "you'd typically keep control until we agree a structure, even in a JV you stay involved") and then walked me into the form. For a first-touch guide that's genuinely useful. She did, tellingly, deflect the "how do you get paid" specifics to "the team will walk through" — same gap as the page.
- The form itself is the best-organised developer intake I've seen on a page like this. Sensibly grouped (details → estate → vision → commercials → agents → title → deal prefs → uploads), microcopy that reassures ("bring what you have, leave the rest"), a Mapbox-backed location autocomplete that actually returned "Busselton, Western Australia" as I typed, lot/plan + deposited-plan upload with a smart explanation of *why* (exact parcel boundary vs indicative), and a submit button correctly disabled until you consent. Native required-field validation caught my empty name/email/estate on submit. This is real craft.
- Two things I'd flag. (1) There's a **Website** field but no **company / ABN** field with a lookup — for a developer or landowner enquiry, the entity and its ABN is basic qualification and you're asking me to type a website instead. (2) The submit button enables as soon as the two consent boxes are ticked, even with the required text fields still blank — it's saved by browser validation, so it's cosmetic, but a developer who mis-reads an enabled button as "ready" gets a small stumble.
- Concrete bug (not user-facing, but real): while interacting I caught a **500 on `f2k-umami.vercel.app/api/send`** — your analytics/Umami beacon is erroring. The Morgan voice endpoint itself returned 200, so this isn't the chat; it's your own instrumentation failing, which means you may be under-counting traffic on this exact page you're trying to measure. Worth a look.
- Opportunity: add a company/ABN field with ABN lookup, and gate the submit button on the required text fields too (not just the checkboxes) so "enabled" always means "submittable."

### Other Strategic Feature Suggestions

- **Show one anonymised proof-of-outcome.** "Seafields: X registrations in Y weeks, Z% converted to EOI" (numbers masked as needed) would validate the entire de-risking promise with evidence instead of assertion. You have the data; a developer weighs case studies over copy.
- **Separate "expression of interest" from "presale" everywhere in the funnel language**, and show the ladder (register → EOI → holding deposit → contract). That ladder is your bridge from marketing to finance and it's currently invisible.
- **A one-line risk-allocation table** for the build (fixed price? escalation? who carries overrun/delay?). It's the single most persuasive thing you could add for my persona and it costs you a paragraph.
- **State the remuneration model in ranges.** Deferring 100% of the money conversation to a call filters out experienced operators, not tyre-kickers.
