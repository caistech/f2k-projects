# Naive-Tester Report — Mobile Marcus
## F2K Funder pages (`/funders` + `/branscombe-estate/funders`)
**Date:** 2026-06-15 · **Persona:** Mobile Marcus (bank relationship manager, iPhone only, fat thumbs, intermittent signal) · **Viewport:** 390×844 (iPhone) · **Method:** live URLs only via /browse, mobile emulation. Screenshots in `./naive-tester-reports/2026-06-15-funders/mobile-marcus/`.

---

Right, I'm a relationship manager at a mutual bank. I got a link to this between two meetings and I'm reading it one-handed on the train. Phone only. If I can't read it or can't tap it, I bounce. Here's what happened.

---

### Section 1 — Landing on `/funders` (the model)

First thing I notice: there's a thin grey disclaimer strip pinned at the very top — "FOR REGISTERED AUSTRALIAN BANKS (ADIs) — Registration of interest only…". Good. As a bank guy that tells me immediately this is aimed at me and it's not a financial-product offer. Tab title reads "For Funders — The F2K funding model | Factory2Key", which is a proper title, not "Create Next App". Tick.

The hero — "Demand is the trigger." — is big and readable, and there's a clear explanatory paragraph under it telling me what this page is (the funding piece of the chain). That's a real explanatory header, not marketing fluff. The page reflows to a single column on my phone, no squashed desktop grid. Good start.

**Friction — the page scrolls sideways.** I caught myself nudging the page left-right with my thumb. Measured it: the page is 438px wide inside a 390px screen, so there's ~48px of horizontal slop. Annoying on a phone — content shouldn't wobble. I traced it to the **Branscombe live-project card** (the cost-stack card), which is 404–428px wide and pokes past the right edge. It's the same culprit on both pages (see Section 5).

**Suggestion:** clamp that card and its inner cost-stack rows to `max-width: 100%` / `100vw - padding` so nothing exceeds the viewport. One overflowing card forces the whole page to scroll sideways.

**Friction — lots of text I have to pinch to read.** The "What F2K delivers" delivery-chain steps (01 through 07) are the worst: the descriptions ("Portal, agents, pre-qualified registrations", "Sea freight (DDP) + port-to-site transport", etc.) are **12px** and the little step numerals are **11px**. There's a whole pile of 11–13px fine print across the page (disclaimers, the "*GST-correct…" notes, the "Indicative · pre-subscription" badges). On the bank's standard for "can I read this without zooming" — I can't, comfortably. I had to pinch.

**Suggestion:** bump body/secondary text to ≥16px on mobile (your own house rule). The 11px numerals especially read as decorative-tiny — fine on a 27" monitor, squint-material on a phone.

**Opportunity:** the "is it a fundable project?" calculator with the three sliders (units / avg price / cost per unit) is genuinely the most engaging thing on the page for me — I'd happily fiddle with it to sanity-check a deal. But it's buried below the delivery chain. Consider surfacing a teaser of it higher up; it's the bit that makes a banker lean in.

### Section 2 — Navigation on mobile

There's a proper hamburger ("Open menu") top-right, and it's a clean **44×44px** tap target — I hit it first time, no fat-thumb misfire. Tapping it drops a tidy list: Projects, Seafields, Branscombe, Hemp Homes, For Developers, Blog, About F2K, plus a "Register interest in a live project →" CTA. Closes again with the same button (now "Close menu"). That's a usable mobile nav pattern. No complaints.

**Opportunity:** the menu opens as a dropdown under the header rather than a full-height drawer. It works, but on a tall iPhone the items sit in the top third and the rest of the screen is dead space. A full-sheet drawer would feel more deliberate. Minor.

---

### Section 3 — Sterling, the voice guide

There's a nicely-presented "Talk to Sterling" card — F2K's funder guide who's meant to explain senior vs junior and walk me through the form. I tapped **"Start a conversation"**.

As flagged, the voice agent isn't wired up yet, so it didn't start a voice call — instead it dropped a **"Type your question" text box with a Send button**. That's the right behaviour: it degrades to a typed fallback rather than throwing an error or a dead button. No console errors. I'll note it and move on — it's a known gap, not a blocker. The text box and Send button are reachable and sit in the page chrome.

**Suggestion:** while voice is unprovisioned, the card still says "🎙️ Start a conversation" with a mic icon — a banker tapping that expects to talk. A small "(text chat for now)" hint under the button would set expectations.

**Opportunity:** Sterling is exactly the right idea for this audience — "senior or junior, what's the difference, what am I committing to" is precisely the hand-holding a relationship manager wants before filling a form. Worth prioritising the voice wiring; it's a differentiator here, not a gimmick.

### Section 4 — Branscombe funder page (`/branscombe-estate/funders`)

Navigated straight to the project page. Tab title: "Fund Branscombe — F2K funding | Factory2Key" — good, project-specific. Explanatory header present and clear: "Claremont TAS · 37 modular dwellings. Register a senior or junior position… Figures are indicative until demand reaches 3× cover. This is a registration of interest only."

The cost-stack-vs-revenue bar chart, the capital-stack ribbon (Senior 50% + five 10% junior tranches), and the "Subscription vs the 3× trigger" progress bar (currently ~1.5×, "≈55 registrations vs 37 lots") all reflow to single column and render fine. As a banker this is the page I actually care about — it shows me the deal shape, the FRoR-for-senior structure, and how far off the trigger they are. Good clarity.

**Same two friction points carry over:** horizontal scroll (438px again, same Branscombe cost-stack card) and the sub-16px fine print (one 10px element, seven at 11px, four at 12px). Fix once, fixes both pages.

**Opportunity:** the "1.5× of the 3× trigger (50% of the way there)" progress bar is a smart trust/FOMO signal for a funder — "demand is building, get in before it's covered." Make it impossible to miss; right now it's mid-page in normal-size type.

---

### Section 5 — THE MAIN EVENT: filling + submitting the registration form (one thumb)

This is what I came to do. Here's the blow-by-blow on a phone.

**Position select (senior / junior radios):** Two big tappable cards — "Senior lender (50% + first right of refusal on the retail mortgage book)" and "Junior lender (10–50%…)". Senior is pre-selected. Each radio's whole card is the hit area — **347×102px** — so there's zero chance of a fat-thumb miss. I tapped **Junior**. Clean.

**The junior % slider:** Selecting Junior *reveals* a percentage slider (10–50%, defaults to 10%) plus a live "indicative participation" readout. I dragged it to **30%** and the readout updated to **$2,400,000 — 30% of the package** in real time. That's exactly right (30% of the $8M package). The slider responds and the money figure is reactive — satisfying, and useful: I can see my cheque size move as I drag. 

  - **Caveat on the slider as a thumb target:** the visible track is only **3px tall**. The thumb is bigger so it's grabbable, but a 3px hairline track is fiddly to land on precisely with a thumb on a moving train — I'd want a chunkier track / bigger thumb hit-area. It worked, but it's the one control I'd call "just barely thumb-friendly" rather than comfortable.

**Text fields:** Bank name, contact name, role/title, email, mobile, division — all full-width, single column, labels wrap (don't truncate). Filled them: *Test Mutual Bank Ltd / Marcus Test / Relationship Manager / funder-test@example.com / 0400000000 / Development finance*. The email field even hints "(bank-domain email expected)", which is a nice touch for the audience. No layout breakage, no zoom-jump on focus.

**The two gating checkboxes:** Both have large tappable label areas (706×80 and 706×46) — easy thumb tap, no collision.
  1. "I confirm [institution] is a registered Australian bank / APRA-authorised ADI, and I am authorised to register this interest on its behalf." — the registered-bank confirmation.
  2. "I understand this is a registration of interest only, is not an offer or invitation, creates no obligation on either side… See the funder terms." — the consent + consequence gate.
  Both ticked fine. **The consequence ("interest only / no obligation") is stated right there before I can submit** — exactly what I want to see as a banker before I put my name to anything.

**Submit:** "Register my interest" button is **292×48px** — tall enough for a thumb, near-full-width of the content column, sits at the bottom where my thumb naturally rests. Reachable, enabled once the required fields + gates are done. Tapped it.

**Result — it worked, end to end.** The form was replaced in place with a confirmation: *"Thank you. We've recorded Test Mutual Bank Ltd's indicative interest in Branscombe as a junior lender. Dennis will be in touch to walk through the term sheet and next steps. This was a registration of interest only — it creates no obligation on either side."* (screenshot `12-confirmation.png`). It echoes back my institution name AND my chosen position (junior), and re-states the no-obligation consequence. No error, no dead-end, no page reload that loses me. That's a clean finish. (Submitted with the test address `funder-test@example.com` so the notification reroutes — not a real bank inbox.)

**Verdict on completability: YES — the form is genuinely fillable and submittable one-handed on a phone.** Every control is a real thumb target, the slider is reactive, the gates are clear, and submit lands a proper confirmation. The only "watch it" is the 3px slider track.

**Opportunity:** the confirmation says "Dennis will be in touch." For a bank that's slightly informal — I'd half-expect "a Factory2Key representative" or at least "Dennis McMahon." Tiny thing, but funders are formal animals.

**Opportunity:** there's an optional PDF upload ("Mandate / term sheet / capacity statement"). On mobile I didn't have a doc to hand — which is normal for "between meetings." Consider a "send the link to my desktop" or "we'll email you a reply-to-attach" path, because the people who *have* the mandate PDF are usually at a desk, not on a phone.
