# Dutton Terrace — Naive-Tester Walkthrough (Anneke)

**Persona:** Anneke, 50s, 25+ years buying and selling rural-town and regional land. Here as a genuine Eyre Peninsula prospect who'd consider a coastal block near Port Lincoln.
**URL:** https://f2k-projects.vercel.app/dutton-terrace-estate
**Date:** 17 June 2026 · Desktop (~1440px) primary, mobile (375px) cross-check
**Verdict up front:** This is a genuinely good concept-stage page. It's honest about what it is (an unzoned idea, not a sales release), it tells me where the town actually is and what living there is like, and the register form is thorough and well-gated. Two real misses: there's no way to *ask a question* (no voice/chat surface), and the satellite map outline is a touch loose. I'd register. I'd just want a human to call me back, and the page doesn't promise that anywhere.

---

## Section-by-section

### Hero + top banner
First thing I see is the dark navy hero with a thin banner across the very top: *"REGISTRATION OF INTEREST ONLY — No deposit is required or accepted. Registering does not create any legal or financial obligation."* As a buyer who's been burned by "register now!" pages that turn into a deposit ask, I appreciate this being the literal first line. It set my guard down immediately.

The hero itself is clean: "Dutton Terrace," "A proposed master-planned community — ~40 family homes alongside a childcare centre and an aged-care facility," then the locator line *"Tumby Bay · South Australia (Eyre Peninsula) · 6.3 ha."* That one line answers my first three questions (what, where, how big) before I've scrolled. Good. A single teal "Register Your Interest →" CTA, no clutter.

**Nitpick:** the hero says "6.3 ha," the stats bar right below says "6.31 ha," and the details table further down says "6.306 ha." All three are technically the same number rounded differently, but a careful buyer notices. Pick a rounding convention (I'd use "6.3 ha" everywhere in prose and "6.306 ha" only in the formal details table) so it doesn't read as sloppy.

### Stats bar
~40 Homes/Lots · 6.31 ha Site Area · Mixed-Use (Resi + Care) · Concept Stage. Scannable, honest, "Concept Stage" right there in the fourth tile so nobody mistakes this for a live release. Fine.

### About the Development
"A community designed around how people actually live." This section earned my trust. It explains the vision (40 homes + childcare + aged-care so families and older residents share a neighbourhood), then plainly states *"The site is currently unzoned and at concept stage. Registering interest now helps us size the right mix of homes and progress planning with real demand behind it."*

That's the right honesty for an unzoned site. I've seen too many regional "estates" marketed as if approvals were a formality. This one tells me up front that it isn't.

The details table is exactly what I want as an experienced buyer: Location, Site Area (6.306 ha), Homes, Land Uses, **Parcel: Allotment 50, Deposited Plan 90582**, **Zoning: Unzoned — rezoning to be progressed**, Stage. Giving me the allotment and DP number is a strong, professional touch — it means I (or my conveyancer) can pull the parcel myself. Most concept pages hide that.

**One mention of the build model:** "Factory2Key delivers the homes as factory-built modular dwellings... the same delivery model proven across our other Western Australian estates." Good that it's stated. But as a buyer this raises a question the page never answers: *what does a factory-built modular home actually look and feel like, and can I see one?* There's no link to the WA estates, no example home, no gallery. See Opportunity below.

### Location — the two maps
This is the section I scrutinised hardest, and it's mostly very good. Two maps side by side on desktop, stacked on mobile:

- **Left ("Where in South Australia"):** a streets/locator map with a red pin on the Eyre Peninsula coast. This does its job — it orients an out-of-towner instantly. Spencer Gulf, the peninsula shape, the pin. Clear.
- **Right ("Indicative extent"):** the corrected **satellite** map. A green dashed/shaded polygon over the cleared paddock on the town edge, red pin centred, "INDICATIVE EXTENT" labelled top-left, street names legible on the tile. The block reads as a real edge-of-town allotment surrounded by houses — which is exactly what a 6.3 ha town-fringe parcel should look like.

The disclaimer below is appropriately careful: *"The outline is an indicative ~6.3 ha extent centred on the site, not a surveyed boundary; the exact parcel (Allotment 50, Deposited Plan 90582) and subdivision layout follow planning confirmation."* Maps credited "© Mapbox © OpenStreetMap." All correct.

**Candid feedback on the outline:** the green overlay is loose. It doesn't hug the obvious cleared-paddock edges — it bleeds slightly past field boundaries on the lower/right side and clips a couple of edge properties. The disclaimer covers you legally ("not a surveyed boundary"), but my eye, which has looked at a *lot* of these, immediately reads it as "they eyeballed a rough box." Since you already have the DP (Allotment 50, DP 90582), tightening the polygon to roughly follow the actual parcel lines would lift the credibility of the whole page a notch. Right now the map says "concept"; a tighter outline would say "concept, but we know our parcel."

**Loading note (likely not a real bug):** on a few of my page loads the satellite tile was slow to paint (it's a heavy static Mapbox image plus three large town JPGs on the page). On a fast connection it's fine, but if a buyer on a patchy regional connection (very common out here) hits the page, the satellite map is the slowest thing to appear. Worth checking the image weight — those town photos are 300–410KB each and the panorama 362KB.

### Living in Tumby Bay
This is the new section and it's the best decision on the page. "A relaxed coastal town, not a commute away." Four info cards:
- **Getting there** — ~50km north of Port Lincoln (daily flights to Adelaide), ~600km from Adelaide by road via Port Augusta. This is *the* question for regional land and you answered it precisely. As a buyer the distance-to-an-airport line matters more than anything else on the page.
- **Schools & childcare** — Tumby Bay Area School, Reception–Year 12 single campus, library on grounds, plus the on-site childcare in the plan.
- **Health & services** — local hospital, pharmacy, Port Lincoln Health Services for the region.
- **Recreation & lifestyle** — the historic jetty (leafy sea-dragon dive site — nice, specific, local detail), Foreshore Park, beaches, boating/fishing, mural-lined main street.

Then two real lifestyle photos — "Foreshore & jetty" and "Main street" — with a proper credit line: *"foreshore & panorama © Inas66 (CC BY-SA 3.0); main street © Bahnfrend (CC BY-SA 4.0), via Wikimedia Commons."* Image credits present and correctly attributed. This section genuinely answers "what's it like to live here" — it's the difference between a land parcel and a *place*.

**Small candour:** the photos are clearly stock/Wikimedia town shots, not the site. That's honest and fine at concept stage, but a line like "photographs are of Tumby Bay town, not the development site" would pre-empt any "is this what the estate looks like?" confusion. Minor.

### The Masterplan Mix
"More than a subdivision." Three cards — Residential (~40 single-family lots), Childcare Centre, Aged-Care Facility — each stamped **PROPOSED**. Below, a clear amber callout: *"Lot plans & pricing coming as planning progresses. The estate is unzoned today..."* Again, the right level of honesty. Nobody can accuse you of overselling. I'm comfortable that "PROPOSED" means proposed.

### Register your interest
Strong explanatory header, then a repeat of the "Registration of Interest only" disclaimer box. The form is thorough and well thought through for a planning-demand exercise:
- Contact (first/last/email required; phone, suburb, postcode optional)
- I'm interested in (land only / house & land / either)
- Preferred lot size (with m² ranges — helpful)
- Indicative budget (land bands and house-&-land bands, plus "Prefer not to say")
- I am a… (first home buyer, downsizer, investor types, govt/essential worker)
- Timeframe, Finance status, How did you hear about us
- **Referrer type** (hard-gated — you must pick before submitting)
- Notes, then a consent checkbox.

**What I liked:**
- The budget helper text: *"Helps us plan a mix people will actually buy — your budget guides the pricing, not the other way around."* That's a genuinely reassuring line for a buyer worried about being upsold. Nicely written.
- The **submit button is disabled until I tick consent** (I verified: empty → disabled; required fields + consent → enabled). Correct consequence-gating.
- The consent checkbox text is complete and compliant — Registration of Interest only, added to the F2K database, may email an acknowledgement, unsubscribe any time. Spam-Act-shaped, good.
- **Submission works.** I filled it as a clearly-fake QA record and it returned a "Thank you" success state — no dead end, no silent failure.

**Friction / candour:**
- **Suburb and postcode are plain text boxes.** Minor, but the portfolio standard for address-type fields is autocomplete. A postcode/suburb autocomplete would also clean your data (people fat-finger postcodes constantly).
- The form is *long*. For a buyer who just wants to say "keep me posted," 12 fields with only 4 required can feel like a credit application. The optional fields are clearly optional, so it's defensible, but consider a one-line nudge near the top: "Only name and email are required — the rest just helps us plan." (You say something like it lower down, but it's buried.)

### Disclaimer + footer
Comprehensive bottom disclaimer ("All information shown... is indicative only and subject to site control, rezoning, planning approval and final confirmation. Nothing here is an offer..."). Footer has factory2key.com.au, a phone number, dennis@factory2key.com.au, Privacy link, ABN-less but it's the marketing footer not a commercial-email send. "Real estate marketing only. No financial product is offered on this site." All appropriate.

## The two things that actually bother me

**1. There is no way to ask a question.** This is my biggest gripe as a real buyer. I have questions a form can't answer: *Can I see one of these modular homes? When does rezoning realistically happen? Is there a display anywhere? What's the body-corporate/strata situation with aged-care next door?* The only floating widget on the page is "Report a problem — get it SayFixed," which is a bug-reporter, not a way to reach a person. There's no voice agent, no chat, no "call us / book a call" CTA beyond the footer email. The whole page funnels me into a form and then... silence. For a high-consideration purchase from an unknown developer, that's a trust gap.

**2. The satellite outline is loose** (covered above) — tighten it to the DP parcel.

---

## Opportunities (strategic)

- **Opportunity — show me a home.** The single biggest unanswered question on the page is "what does a Factory2Key modular home actually look like?" One photo or a link to a finished WA estate home would do more for conversion than anything else. Right now I'm asked to register interest in homes I literally cannot picture.
- **Opportunity — a voice/chat clarifier on the register section.** A buyer mid-form has exactly the nuanced questions a label can't answer (lot timing, finance, the care facilities). A voice agent or even a "Questions? Talk to us" surface would catch the people who abandon the long form.
- **Opportunity — set the expectation of a reply.** After "Thank you," tell me what happens next and when ("We'll email you as planning milestones are reached; expect the first update within X"). A success screen that just says thanks leaves a keen buyer wondering if anyone's actually on the other end.
- **Opportunity — tighten the ha figure** to one consistent rounding in prose.
- **Opportunity — label the town photos** as Tumby Bay town (not the site) to remove any ambiguity.
